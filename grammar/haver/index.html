Haver

Haver is irregular and appears constantly in European Portuguese — most often as impersonal há (“there is / there are”), but also in compound tenses and set phrases.

What you hear every day
  • Há — there is / there are · time passed (“há dois anos”)
  • Há de — will probably (eu hei de ir…)
  • Havia — there was / used to be (narrative, background)
  • With ter + havido — compound tenses (têm havido muitos acidentes…)

Impersonal há — existence & time

No subject pronoun: the verb is always third person.

Há um café perto daqui. · Há muitas pessoas na rua.
There is a café nearby. · There are many people on the street.
Há muito tempo que não nos víamos.
It has been a long time since we saw each other.
Há quatro janelas na sala.
There are four windows in the living room.

Past & future (snapshot)

Houve muitas oportunidades. · Havia muita especulação no mercado.
There were many opportunities. · There was a lot of speculation in the market.
Haverá tempo para brincar amanhã. · Haverá muitas pessoas na festa.
There will be time to play tomorrow. · There will be lots of people at the party.

Full conjugation tables (conditional, compound tenses with havido) are on the verb list — this page focuses on patterns you meet in conversation.

Haver vs ter

Ter = to have (ownership, age, hunger: tenho 30 anos, temos fome). Haver = existence / “there is”, time elapsed, and auxiliary in some compound forms. Do not use haver for “I have a car” → Tenho um carro, not *Hei um carro.

Há is not “have” in English. Há dois cafés = there are two cafés, not “I have two cafés”.
