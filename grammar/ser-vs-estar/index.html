Ser vs estar

Portuguese has two verbs for “to be”. Choosing the right one is one of the first big hurdles — but the patterns repeat.

A1

Quick rule
SerEstar
Identity, nationality, professionLocation (where someone is now)
Defining traits (often long-lasting)Temporary feelings and moods
What something is (type, quality in general)Changeable state — how things are right now

“Permanent vs temporary” is a useful shortcut, but meaning and context matter more — see below.

Ser — who / what / when

Use ser for identity, origin, profession, and traits you present as part of who someone is — not where they happen to be at the moment.

Ele é arquiteto. · Nós somos portugueses. · Eu sou professora.
He is an architect. · We are Portuguese. · I am a teacher.
Eu sou loira. · Ele é de Lisboa. · São três horas.
I am blond. · He is from Lisbon. · It is three o'clock.
O café é caro, mas é bom.
The coffee is expensive, but it is good. (general quality)

Personality and character often take ser when you mean a general trait:

Eu sou ansioso.
I am an anxious person. (usually / by nature)

Estar — where / how (now)

Use estar for location, health, mood, hunger, and situations that can change — even if they last a while.

Estou em casa. · Ele está na cozinha. · Ela está cansada.
I am at home. · He is in the kitchen. · She is tired.
O café está quente. · Nós estamos com fome. · Eu estou desempregado.
The coffee is hot. (right now) · We are hungry. · I am unemployed. (current situation)
Como estás? — Estou bem, obrigada.
How are you? — I am fine, thanks.

The same adjective with estar often means a passing feeling:

Eu estou ansioso.
I am feeling anxious. (right now)

Same adjective, different meaning

Some adjectives change meaning depending on the verb:

With serWith estar
é ansioso — he is an anxious personestá ansioso — he is feeling anxious now
é mau — he is a bad personestá mau — he is unwell
é verde — it is green (colour)está verde — it is unripe
é seguro — it is safe (in general)está seguro — he feels safe

Common mistakes

  • Do not use ser for where someone is: Estou em Lisboa, not *Sou em Lisboa.
  • Do not use estar for nationality or profession: Sou português, not *Estou português.
  • English “to be” does not always map to the same verb — check whether you mean identity/type or current state.
  • With adjectives, sou vs estou can change the meaning entirely (sou ansioso vs estou ansioso).

Progressive actions

Ongoing actions use estar + a + infinitive in European Portuguese (not gerund like Brazilian PT):

Estou a estudar português. · Eles estão a jantar.
I am studying Portuguese. · They are having dinner.
Tip: Memory hook: Ser = essence (who/what). Estar = state (where/how now). Location almost always takes estar: Lisboa é bonita (in general) but Estou em Lisboa (I am there now).

Conjugation

Ser is irregular in every tense; estar is regular in the present (and still worth learning in other tenses). In speech you may hear shortened forms like tá for está — fine in conversation, not in formal writing.
