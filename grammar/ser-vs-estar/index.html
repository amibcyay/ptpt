Ser vs estar

Portuguese has two verbs for “to be”. Choosing the right one is one of the first big hurdles — but the patterns repeat.

Quick rule
  • Ser — identity, origin, profession, permanent traits, time/date, what something is.
  • Estar — location, temporary state, mood, health, ongoing situation, how something seems right now.

Ser — who / what / when

Eu sou professora. · Ele é de Lisboa. · São três horas.
I am a teacher. · He is from Lisbon. · It is three o'clock.
O café é caro, mas é bom.
The coffee is expensive, but it is good. (general quality)

Estar — where / how (now)

Estou em casa. · Ela está cansada. · O café está quente.
I am at home. · She is tired. · The coffee is hot. (right now)
Como estás? — Estou bem, obrigada.
How are you? — I am fine, thanks.

Same adjective, different meaning

Some adjectives change meaning depending on the verb:

With serWith estar
é mau — he is a bad personestá mau — he is unwell
é verde — it is green (colour)está verde — it is unripe
é seguro — it is safe (in general)está seguro — he feels safe

Progressive actions

Ongoing actions use estar + a + infinitive in European Portuguese (not gerund like Brazilian PT):

Estou a estudar português. · Eles estão a jantar.
I am studying Portuguese. · They are having dinner.
Memory hook: Ser = essence (who/what). Estar = state (where/how now). Location almost always takes estar: Lisboa é bonita (in general) but Estou em Lisboa (I am there now).
