Cognate patterns

English and Portuguese share thousands of Latin-derived words. Ending patterns often match — which speeds up reading — but watch for spelling and gender shifts.

A1

Patterns that often work

English endingPortugueseExamples
-tion-ção (f.)information → a informação
-ity-idade (f.)university → a universidade
-ous-oso / -osafamous → famoso, famosa
-ly (adverb)-mentequickly → rapidamente
-al-alnational → nacional
-ent / -ant-ente / -antedifferent → diferente

Verb lookalikes (-ar verbs)

English verbs in -ate often become Portuguese -ar:

communicate → comunicar · participate → participar · organise → organizar
Stress and vowels change, but recognition is instant.

Same spelling, different part of speech

Verb: Eles circulam pela cidade. · Adj.: Uma estrada circular.
They move around the city. · A ring road.

Spelling shifts to remember

  • ph → f: phone → telefone
  • ct → ct or just c: action → ação
  • ss / s / ç: necessary → necessário
  • Silent letters drop: psychology → psicologia
False friends look related but mean something else — e.g. preservativo (condom), excitado (aroused, not “excited” in the English sense). Always check meaning, not just shape.
Tip: Use cognates to guess at text, then confirm with context. Pair with False friends (EP).
