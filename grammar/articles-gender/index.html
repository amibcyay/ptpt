Articles & gender

Every noun in Portuguese is masculine or feminine. The article (o, a, os, as) must agree.

Definite articles

SingularPlural
Masculineo livroos livros
Femininea casaas casas

Indefinite articles

SingularPlural
Masculineum livrouns livros
Feminineuma casaumas casas

Guessing gender from the ending

  • Often feminine: ends in -a (a mesa, a porta)
  • Often masculine: ends in -o (o gato, o carro)
  • Also common: -ção → feminine (a nação); -or → often masculine (o professor)
Endings are hints, not rules. O dia, a mão, o problema break the pattern — learn gender with the word.

When EP uses the definite article

European Portuguese uses o/a more often than English uses “the”:

Gosto do café. · A Maria telefonou. · Vou para a escola.
I like coffee. · Maria called. · I am going to school.
  • Before names in familiar speech: a Ana, o João
  • With many country names: o Canadá, o Brasil
  • After gostar de, the noun often keeps its article: gosto do cinema

When you often skip the article

Sou professora. · Ele é médico.
I’m a teacher. · He’s a doctor. — no article with professions after ser.

Contractions (very common)

Prepositions merge with articles:

de + articleem + articlea + article
do, da, dos, dasno, na, nos, nasao, à, aos, às
Venho da escola. · Estou no café. · Vou ao mercado.
I come from school. · I am at the café. · I am going to the market.
When you learn a new noun, always learn it with its article: a mesa, not just mesa.
