Present tense — regular verbs

The present indicative (presente do indicativo) describes habits, facts, and actions happening now. This is what the verb conjugations page shows for hundreds of common A1–B2 verbs.

A1

Three conjugation groups

  • -ar verbs — falar, trabalhar, estudar
  • -er verbs — comer, beber, vender
  • -ir verbs — partir, abrir, decidir

Stem changes (still “regular” in textbooks)

Many verbs tweak the vowel in the stem for eu, tu, ele, eles but keep the same endings:

poder → posso, podes, pode · comer → como · sentir → sinto, sentes, sente
These are predictable once you know the pattern — check each verb on the list.

Regular vs irregular — quick overview

Regular verbs keep the same stem and take the -ar / -er / -ir endings above. Irregular verbs change the stem or use special forms (ser → sou, ir → vou, ter → tenho). Learn regular patterns first, then the high-frequency irregulars on the irregular-present and core-verbs pages.

Rule of thumb
  • If the dictionary lists a normal -ar/-er/-ir infinitive and no “irregular” note → use the endings on this page.
  • Common irregulars to learn early: ser, estar, ir, ter, fazer, haver, dizer, dar, ver, saber, querer, poder.
  • Stem-changing verbs (pedir → peço) are often still taught as “regular with a stem tweak” — endings stay the same.

What the present can mean

  • Habit: Trabalho em casa. — I work from home.
  • Right now: Como sopa. — I am eating soup.
  • Future (informal): Amanhã vou ao médico. — Tomorrow I am going to the doctor.
